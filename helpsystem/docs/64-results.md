# Results

Ranking:  
one row per solvent, with the solubility at the hot and at the cold temperature, the cooling yield, the activity coefficient of the solute at both temperatures, the ICH Q3C class and concentration limit of the solvent, and notes. The solvent that dissolves most at the hot temperature comes first. The cooling yield is the fraction of the dissolved solute that crystallizes when a solution saturated at the hot temperature is cooled to the cold one, with the mass of solvent constant.

Solubility curves:  
the solubility against temperature in every solvent, as a chart and as a table.

Antisolvent:  
the solubility against the mass fraction of antisolvent in the solvent mixture, and the fraction of the solute that crystallizes when antisolvent is added to a solution saturated in the pure solvent. That fraction is negative while the mixture dissolves more than the dilution takes away.

Measured:  
each measured solubility next to the value of the model at the same temperature, with the deviation and the two activity coefficients.

Messages:  
the fusion data used for the solute, the solvents left out by the filter, and warnings.

A solvent shows **melt** where the temperature is at or above the melting point of the solute.
