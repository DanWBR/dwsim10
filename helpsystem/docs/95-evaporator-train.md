# Evaporator Train

A multiple-effect black liquor evaporator in one block. Live steam heats the first effect, the vapour of each effect condenses in the next one, and the vapour of the last effect goes to the surface condenser. The liquor runs backward (it enters the last effect) or forward (it enters the first one). In each effect the liquor boils at the boiling point rise of the package at the pressure of the effect; the saturation temperatures of the effects are evenly spaced between the first and the last effect pressures. The block solves the heat balances of all the effects at once (Newton), with the enthalpies of the package, for the evaporation of each effect and, when the strong liquor dry solids is the specification, for the steam, which it then writes into the steam stream. The other specification takes the steam as it comes and gives the dry solids it reaches. The steam condensate leaves saturated, and the condensates of the effects and of the surface condenser leave mixed.

**Results:** steam, evaporation, steam economy, steam heat, surface condenser duty, strong liquor solids and temperature, and for each effect the pressure, boiling temperature, boiling point rise, dry solids, evaporation and temperature difference. If an effect has less than 3 K between the condensing vapour and the boiling liquor, the block says so.

With a eucalyptus liquor at 14 % and six effects from 1.9 to 0.135 bar heated by steam at 4.5 bar, backward feed reaches 80 % with an economy of 4.4 and 8 to 12.6 K in every effect; the energy balance closes within 0.1 kW of 28.6 MW. Forward feed would need less steam, but its last effect has to boil the liquor at 80 % at 0.135 bar and runs out of temperature difference, which is why mills feed the weak liquor backward. The heat transfer areas aren’t calculated, and the condensates and the liquor aren’t flashed between the effects.
