# Minimum miscibility pressure

The search follows the multiple mixing cell method of Ahmadi and Johns. At one pressure, the injection gas and the oil are mixed and flashed. The liquid then meets fresh gas, upstream, and the vapour meets fresh oil, downstream; each contact that splits in two phases adds a tie line, and a mixture that stays in one phase becomes one more cell. After enough contacts the cells hold the tie lines that control the displacement. The length of the shortest one,


\[
L=\sqrt{\sum_{i}\left(y_{i}-x_{i}\right)^{2}},
\]


falls as the pressure rises and reaches zero at the minimum miscibility pressure. When the length is still changing after the last contact, its limit for an infinite number of contacts is taken from an extrapolation in the number of contacts.

The search goes up in pressure from the first one given, and finds the answer by extrapolating $L^{n}$ linearly to zero, with the exponent $n$ that makes the last points most nearly linear, and by halving the interval once a miscible pressure is found. Where the shortest tie line sits tells the drive: next to the oil for a vaporizing drive, next to the gas for a condensing one, and in the middle for the combined condensing and vaporizing drive of rich gases and of carbon dioxide.

In a vaporizing drive the result does not depend on the gas: it is the pressure at which the tie line that extends through the oil becomes critical, so nitrogen and methane give about the same value for a given oil.
